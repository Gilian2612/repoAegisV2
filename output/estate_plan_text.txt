LIVING TRUST AND LAST WILL & TESTAMENT
OUTLINE
The following outline briefly describes the provisions of your Revocable Living Trust and your Last Will and Testament.  If you have any additional questions or if you need further clarification, please do not hesitate to contact me.
Revocable Living Trust
PARAGRAPH 1
The first paragraph defines the parties of the Trust and revokes all your previous Trusts.  You are the Settlor, which means you created the Trust.  You are also the Trustee,  which gives you total control over the Trust.
ARTICLE I - FUNDING OF TRUST
This Article gives you as the Settlor the power to transfer your assets to the Trust ("Fund the Trust") and allows you as the Trustee to accept the assets and manage them.
ARTICLE II - TRUST AMENDMENT AND REVOCATION
This Article gives you the power to change, amend, or revoke the Trust at any time or in any manner.  You can change one provision of the Trust or you can decide to change the whole document.
ARTICLE III - ADMINISTRATION DURING THE LIFETIME OF THE SETTLOR
This Article entitles you to the benefits of your assets in the Trust, as if you owned the assets in your name alone.  You are entitled to all the income of the Trust, as well as the principal, in any amounts that you require.  Additionally, this Article provides language necessary to ensure that you are able to retain your Homestead tax exemption.  Such language notates that you are entitled to full use and possession of your residence.
ARTICLE IV - INCAPACITY OF THE SETTLOR
If you become incapacitated and can not manage your own affairs, then your Successor Trustee, who is later named, will manage your accounts and use your money for your benefit.  You will be considered incapacitated if your attending physician, a judge, or two certified physicians determine that you are incapacitated.
ARTICLE V - PAYMENT OF THE SETTLOR'S DEBTS, FUNERAL EXPENSES AND TAXES
All your final expenses, such as funeral, medical, taxes, etc., should be paid out of Trust.
ARTICLE VI - DISTRIBUTION OF TRUST REMAINDER UPON DEATH OF THE SETTLOR
Upon your death, your Trust assets will be distributed according to the provisions in this Article.
ARTICLE VII - PROVISION REGARDING MINORS
If any of your beneficiaries are under the age of twenty-five (25), then their share shall not be given to them outright, but continued in Trust.  Your Successor Trustee, during the term of the Trust, can use the money for their support, including college.  This provision protects the assets from being spent foolishly when your beneficiaries are too young to handle their own finances.  When the beneficiary turns twenty-five (25) they are entitled to whatever amount is left in the Trust.
ARTICLE VIII - PROVISIONS FOR DISABLED BENEFICIARIES
If income or principal is payable to a beneficiary who is unable to manage their own affairs, then the Trustee can use their share of the assets to pay directly to persons caring for that beneficiary or to establish a Special Needs Trust for their benefit.
ARTICLE IX - SPENDTHRIFT PROVISION
Any assets that are continued in Trust, as provided for in the above Articles, will be protected from your beneficiary's creditors.  This means if your beneficiary owes large sums of money to a creditor, that creditor is not entitled to the assets in the Trust, only what is actually distributed to the beneficiary.
ARTICLE X - SIMULTANEOUS DEATH
This provision protects your wishes by not allowing your beneficiary's Trust or Will to control who receives your assets in the event of a simultaneous death or common disaster event.  If one of your beneficiaries dies within thirty days of your death or under circumstances where it cannot be determined who died first, that beneficiary is deemed to not have survived you.  This is important because if your beneficiary is deemed to have survived you, then the money goes to that beneficiary's Estate and the money will pass through that beneficiary's Trust or Will and not yours.
ARTICLE XI - PROVISIONS REGARDING TRUSTEE
Upon your death or incapacity, you are naming Maria Desiree Suarez Guevara, to act as Successor Trustee, or take your place over the management of the assets.  If Maria Desiree Suarez Guevara, cannot act, then Rafael Eduardo Suarez Torres will act.  In the event that either person is unable or unwilling to act, then the remaining named person shall act alone.
The remaining provisions of the Article deal with administrative powers of the Trustees.  They are not required to post a bond, they may resign at any time, and they may prepare accountings to the beneficiaries if they feel it is necessary.
ARTICLE XII - PROVISIONS FOR RETIREMENT ACCOUNTS
This Article enables you to pass retirement assets from a qualified retirement account upon your death to your beneficiaries according to your wishes and the goals you provided for through your Trust and to continue to maintain the same tax advantages.
ARTICLE XIII - POWERS OF TRUSTEES
This Article gives you and your Successor Trustee the power to use your assets for your benefit.  Any power or authority you have over assets in your name alone, such as to buy, sell, borrow against, lease, etc., you have for your assets in the Trust.
ARTICLE XIV - GOVERNING LAW
The laws of Florida will govern your Trust.
ARTICLE XV - TRUSTEES ACTIONS BINDING
This Article states that anyone dealing with you as Trustees is not required to determine whether the Trust is valid and whether you have the authority to act.

THIS TRUST AGREEMENT, executed on _______________, at Seminole County, Florida, by and between Maria Dayana Suarez, hereinafter called the "Settlor," and Maria Dayana Suarez, hereinafter called the "Trustee," hereby revoking and declaring null and void any and all Trusts and Amendments by the Settlor at any time heretofore made.
As of this date, the Settlor is single.
As of this date, the Settlor has no children.
ARTICLE I - FUNDING OF TRUST
The Settlor hereby transfers to the Trustee the property described on Schedule A attached hereto, in Trust, and the Trustee accepts such property, and any additions thereto which are acceptable to the Trustee, and agrees to hold, manage, and distribute the same upon the terms set forth in this Agreement.
This Trust shall be known as the "Maria Dayana Suarez Revocable Trust Agreement dated _______________."
ARTICLE II - TRUST AMENDMENT AND REVOCATION
At any time or times during the life of the Settlor, by an instrument in writing, signed by the Settlor and delivered to the Trustee, the Settlor may:
Amend, alter, modify or change this Trust Agreement in any manner; and/or
Change the Successor Trustee or Successor Trustees; and/or
Withdraw any part or all of the properties contained in the Trust; and/or
Revoke or terminate this Trust Agreement, in part or in whole, in which latter event any and all Trust properties shall forthwith revert to the Settlor as his/her own property free of Trust.
Furthermore, for purposes of this Article, delivery on any Trustee is sufficient in lieu of delivery on all Trustee(s).
ARTICLE III - ADMINISTRATION DURING THE LIFETIME OF THE SETTLOR
During the Settlor's lifetime, the Trustee may distribute all income and principal of the Trust Estate to the Settlor in such amount or amounts as the Settlor may direct. All undistributed income may be accumulated.
In addition, for the Settlor's lifetime, the Settlor shall be entitled to full use and possession of any non-income producing real estate owned by the Trust, specifically including the Settlor's residence.  The Settlor shall also be permitted to be the named insured under any homeowner's insurance policy on property owned by the Settlor.
ARTICLE IV - INCAPACITY OF THE SETTLOR
If at any time the Settlor is unable to manage his/her affairs, the Trustee may use such sums from the income and principal of the Trust Estate as the Trustee deems necessary or advisable for the care, support and comfort of the Settlor.
For purposes of this Agreement, the Settlor shall be considered to be unable to manage his/her affairs if the Settlor is under a legal disability or by reason of illness or mental or physical disability is incapacitated to act rationally and prudently in the Settlor's own financial best interests, and the determination as to Settlor's inability at any time shall be made by the Settlor's then attending physician or two licensed physicians (each of whom represents that he/she is certified by a recognized medical board), and the Trustee or any Successor Trustee may rely upon written notice of that determination.
ARTICLE V - PAYMENT OF THE SETTLOR'S DEBTS, FUNERAL EXPENSES AND TAXES
Upon the Settlor's death, the Successor Trustee shall pay from the residue of the Trust Estate the Settlor's funeral expenses, costs of administration, and Estate and inheritance taxes assessed by reason of the Settlor's death.  The Trustee may make payment directly or to the Settlor's Personal Representative.  The Settlor hereby waives all rights of reimbursement for any payments made pursuant to this Article.
ARTICLE VI - DISTRIBUTION OF TRUST REMAINDER UPON DEATH OF THE SETTLOR
Upon the death of the Settlor, the Successor Trustee shall distribute the rest, residue and remainder of the Trust Estate in equal shares to the following beneficiaries (each, a “Named Beneficiary”), per the schedule below, if living; and if any Named Beneficiary is not living, then that Named Beneficiary’s share shall be distributed to his or her issue, in equal shares, per stirpes:
Maria Desiree Suarez Guevara: 50% of the assets in the Trust Estate
Rafael Eduardo Suarez Torres: 25% of the assets in the Trust Estate
Gioconda Del Carmen Guevara: 25% of the assets in the Trust Estate
If any Named Beneficiary is not living at the time of distribution and leaves no issue, then that deceased Named Beneficiary’s share shall be distributed pro rata among the remaining Named Beneficiaries who are then living; provided, however, that if none of the Named Beneficiaries is then living, such share shall be distributed to the issue of the Named Beneficiaries, in equal shares, per stirpes.
ARTICLE VII - PROVISION REGARDING MINORS
Each share of the Trust Estate which is distributable to a beneficiary who has not reached the age of twenty-five (25) years shall immediately vest in the beneficiary, but the Successor Trustee shall retain possession of the share as a separate Trust until the beneficiary reaches the age of twenty-five (25) years, meanwhile paying to or for the benefit of the beneficiary so much or all of the income and principal of the share as the Successor Trustee deems necessary for the beneficiary's health, maintenance, education and best interests, and adding to principal any income not so paid.
In administering distributions under this Article for a beneficiary who is a minor, if a legal guardian of the person has been appointed or is serving for such minor (including a guardian nominated by the Settlor and appointed by a court of competent jurisdiction), the Successor Trustee may, in the Successor Trustee’s discretion, make payments and distributions that otherwise would be made to or for the benefit of the minor by making such payments and distributions to that guardian, to be applied by the guardian for the minor’s benefit.
ARTICLE VIII - PROVISIONS FOR DISABLED BENEFICIARIES
1. In the event any distribution shall become payable to a person other than the Settlor under a legal disability, or a person not adjudged incapacitated but who, by reason of illness,  substance abuse, excessive gambling or other mental or physical disability, is, in the opinion of the Trustee, unable to properly administer such amounts ("Disabled Beneficiary"), then the Trustee is authorized, in his, her, their, or its absolute discretion, to retain all or part of such distribution and to pay over and distribute all or part of such income and principal for the suitable support, care and maintenance of such person in such of the following ways as the Trustee shall deem best:
directly to such beneficiary;
to the legally-appointed guardian or conservator of such beneficiary;
to some person or persons having the care of such beneficiary for his or her suitable support, maintenance, welfare, and education;
by the direct application of such amounts for the suitable support, maintenance, welfare, and education of such beneficiary; or
to a Supplemental or Special Needs Trust for the benefit of the Disabled Beneficiary.
The Trustee is authorized to require the Disabled Beneficiary to submit to the Trustee for review appropriate evidence as to the existence or non-existence of a compelling reason to retain all or part of such distribution to a Disabled Beneficiary.
2.  As it pertains to Paragraph 1(E), if the Disabled Beneficiary's disability is of such a nature that they are eligible for any state, local or governmental benefits, assistance and eligibility is conditioned on certain income or resource limitations, then the Trustee may, in his, her, their or its absolute discretion, administer, distribute and pay over as much income and/or principal to a Supplemental or Special Needs Trust or pursuant to the sections below.
The Trustee shall pay to or for the benefit of the Disabled Beneficiary, such amounts from the income and principal of the Trust held for his or her benefit as the Trustee in his, her, their, or its sole and absolute discretion considers to be necessary or advisable.  It is the Settlor's intent that trust income and principal be used for the satisfaction of the Disabled Beneficiary's special needs. Any undistributed income of the Trust may be added to principal at the discretion of the Successor Trustee.

COMMONLY ASKED QUESTIONS ABOUT
ESTATE PLANNING AND
LIVING WITH YOUR LIVING TRUST
Q.  What happens after I complete the initial transfer of my assets to the Trust?
A.  Life goes on as normal.  If you are the Trustee and you want to sell a Trust asset, you sell the asset as if you owned it yourself.  If you want to buy a new asset, you buy it in the name of the Trust.  It makes no sense to buy it in your name and then have to have it re-transferred to the Trust's name later.  The point is that nothing in your life really changes while you are your own Trustee, except that you must remember to have your assets registered in the Trust's name.  This is really not difficult or bothersome once you get used to it.
Q.  Why do banks and brokers want to keep copies of my Trust?
A.  They are not nosy about your affairs.  What they are trying to do is to protect you and your Trust beneficiaries by making sure that when you pass away, they are not dealing with a person who is not named in the Trust.  They also want to see the Trust to ensure that the Trustee has the power to make certain investments.
Q.  I told my broker the full name of my Trust, "John Doe, as Trustee of the John Doe Revocable Trust Agreement dated 12/1/11," but it came back "John Doe TTEE U/A of 12/1/11."  Is this OK?
A.  Yes.  Each stock transfer agent has his own abbreviation format.  Most will use the full legal terminology but some will not.  As long as the name of the Trustee, some form of the word "Trust," and the date of the Trust appears on the title, it will suffice.
Q.  Must I sign "Trustee" after my name on all transactions involving the Trust?
A.  No.  You merely sign your own name.  Most institutions will not require you to sign as Trustee, but will tell you if it is needed.
Q.  If I change my certificate of deposit from my name to my Trust's name before it is due, will I be charged with an early withdrawal penalty?
A.  As long as you're leaving the funds on deposit with the financial institution, most banks will not charge an early withdrawal penalty for changing the registration to your Trust.   However, you should make sure to ask your bank before changing the name to your Trust.
Q.  I went to my banker and broker to change my IRA to my Trust's name but they said that was impossible.  What happened?
A.  Remember, you cannot change ownership of IRA assets to your Trust's name.  You can, however, change the beneficiary of your IRA to your Trust by getting a change of beneficiary form. Also, remember to carefully check the income tax issues of your IRA with a CPA or attorney before making any beneficiary change.
Q.  If I want to change my Trust, how can I do it?
A.  You must have a separate short legal document, called a Trust Amendment, prepared by your attorney.  Do not write on your original Trust or attempt to change it on your own.  Trust Amendments do not cost as much as a new Trust and will save litigation costs later, if they are properly done.
Q: What records should I keep for the Trust if I am my own Trustee?
A: Since you are both the Trustee and beneficiary, you do not have to give an accounting to yourself. However, you should keep some central list of the Trust assets and account numbers, including life insurance policies, user names and passwords, safe deposit information, and names of your advisors (CPA, attorney, stockbroker, financial planner, insurance salesman, etc.) in a place known to your Successor Trustee, so he or she can find it and begin to take over should you become incapacitated or pass away.
Q.  If I am my own Trustee, what happens when I die?
A.  Your Successor Trustee obtains several copies of your Death Certificate and contacts your banks and brokers, telling them that he or she is now in charge of the Trust.  He or she proves this by giving each one a certified copy of your Death Certificate and a copy of the Trust.  After your Successor Trustee gets control of the Trust assets, he or she then pays your bills and distributes the Trust in accordance with your instructions spelled out in the Trust.  He or she also must keep records and account to your beneficiaries and the IRS.  (Note: your Successor Trustee does the same thing if you become incapacitated.  Instead of a Death Certificate, he or she proves his or her authority to act by having your physician write a letter, stating you cannot function as a Trustee).  It will probably be a good idea for your Successor Trustee to consult with an attorney before administrating the Trust at your death, to make sure everything is done properly.
Q.  What work does the attorney do after my death if I have a living Trust?
A.  There may be several, or absolutely no duties, for the attorney.  The attorney's duties might consist of any or all of the following:
Advising the Successor Trustee as to what his or her responsibilities are in carrying out the provisions of the Trust.
Explaining to the Trust beneficiaries the provisions of the Trust.

Q.  What will the attorney charge for these services?
A.  It is difficult to quote a fee for these services, as they vary in each case.  Many times, there is no attorney's fee at all.  The point to keep in mind is that the better job that you do in the funding your Trust, and the better businessman that your Trustee is, the less work (and fees) there is for your attorney.
Q.  Where should I keep my original Will and Trust?
A.  Your Will and Trust should be protected, yet accessible to your Successor Trustee when needed. I recommend the original documents be kept in a safe deposit box.  You should make sure someone other than you has access to the box or re-title the box into the name of your Trust.  It is very important that your original documents are not lost.
Q.  Should my Successor Trustee have a copy of my Trust?
A.  If your Successor Trustee is also a beneficiary, I do not recommend that he or she be given a copy of the Trust.  Instead, tell him or her that he or she is to take over your Trust if you get sick or pass away.  Tell him or her where he or she can get your Trust papers and records.  Also tell him or her who your attorney is and recommend that he or she contact that attorney for advice.  If your Successor Trustee is not a beneficiary of the Trust, however, then it does not hurt for the Successor Trustee to have a copy of the Trust.
Q.  What does "otherwise to his/her issue in equal shares, per stirpes" mean in my Trust and Will?
A.  The term "issue" refers to one's lineal descendants, such as children.  "Per stirpes" means by the bloodline, so the phrase states that if one of your beneficiaries should predecease you, his or her share would go to his or her children, equally.  This prevents your beneficiary's share from going to his or her spouse, or to your step-children, and ensures that it stays within that person's bloodline if he or she predeceases you.
Q.  What should I put on my Schedule "A?"
A.  The page referred to as the Schedule "A" in your Trust allows for you to keep track of the assets that you transfer, or re-title, into the name of your Trust.  This way, your Successor Trustee knows what assets the Trust holds when he or she must step into your place.  However, merely listing an asset on the Schedule does not automatically re-title its ownership.  You must contact each individual institution to re-title your accounts as outlined in the Instructions for Funding and Administration of Revocable Trust memorandum included with your drafts.
Q.  What is the "Written Statement" and when do I need to complete it?
A.  The Written Statement is found on the last page or your Last Will and Testament. This allows you create a list of how you wish to distribute specific pieces your tangible personal property such as jewelry, clothing, or furniture. This form can be completed or added to at any time but must be signed to take effect.  This form does not require witnesses or a notary to be legal.
Q.  Does my motor vehicle go through probate?
A.  Under Florida law, a formal estate administration is not required in order to transfer ownership of a motor vehicle.  The transfer can be done at the tax collector's office in the county in which the decedent resided.
Q.  What does "Attorney in Fact" mean?
A.  Your Attorney in Fact is the person whom you have designated as your Power of Attorney within the document itself.  This allows your spouse, your children, or whomever you have chosen, to act on your behalf as your agent.  The term Attorney in Fact does not refer to the lawyer who prepared your Durable Family Power of Attorney, but rather, the person(s) appointed by you therein.
Q.  Why don’t I have a document called "Living Will?"
A.  The term "Living Will" is the everyday reference to your document entitled "Declaration Under Florida Life Prolonging Procedure Act."  It is a shorter and easier title for that document but means the same thing.
Q.  Can I amend my Trust myself?
A.  Under Florida law, you are allowed to amend your own Trust.  However, in order for the changes to be valid the amendment will need to be executed in accordance with the same formalities as your original Trust (i.e., typed, signed in the presence of two uninterested parties and notarized).  You cannot simply handwrite and initial the changes on your document.  While you can always prepare your own amendments, I recommend reaching out to the firm to make those changes so we can ensure the changes are both compliant with Florida law and in the best interest of you and your Estate.
Q. Do beneficiary designations on financial accounts supersede the Trust?
A. When a financial account is set up, such as a bank account or an investment account, you may have the ability to list a designated beneficiary. By designating beneficiaries on an account, you are indicating you want that account to go to that beneficiary upon your passing. Doing this will prevent that account from going through probate. With that said, if you have accounts in your name with designated beneficiaries directly on the account, then those accounts are not in your Trust and are not governed by the terms of your Trust. This means when you pass, whoever is designated on that account will receive those funds outright, despite what is written in your Trust. Whether or not you should retitle your accounts into the Trust, or just list designated beneficiaries on the accounts is something you should discuss with your attorney.
Q. How do I know if I have designated beneficiaries on my accounts?
A. You have to contact your financial institutions and see how the account is set up. Common abbreviations that indicate designated beneficiaries include POD (“pay on death”), TOD (“transfer on death”), and ITF (“in trust for”)
